# Contents
## Introduction
Sail & Broadside is a tabletop miniatures game set in a fictional world played using models of sailing ships at 1:700 scale. The gameplay is heavily influenced by your ability to make the most of the wind and your positioning. All dice roles in Sail & Broadside are done using twenty-sided dice (D20.) Each player has a squadron of ships that they maneuver to try to complete their objectives in a scenario and to defeat their opponent.
## What You Need to Play
To play Sail & Broadside you will need several things in addition to this book: a table to play on, miniature ships, a measuring device in inches, three quarter circle turning arcs, a handful of twenty-sided dice (D20), and a squadron list. Additionally, you will want a selection of terrain pieces to represent the scenery of Sail & Broadside. In case you are new to tabletop wargaming these things are explained in the next sections.
### Miniatures
Miniatures are essentially models of plastic, resin, or metal at a scale smaller than 1:1. For Sail & Broadside the scale is 1:700 while many common tabletop games focused on individuals, rather than ships, are 28mm or ~1:56. Most miniatures are sold unpainted, and often unassembled. Painting and assembling models is a large part of the hobby experience for many wargamers.
Sail & Broadside was designed around 1:700 scale and the rules are designed for play using the base sizes from the chart below.

### Dice
Most tabletop games use dice to determine the outcomes of your actions. Sail & Broadside is designed around using dice with 20 sides or “D20”. For a smooth game experience, I recommend you have three sets of four D20s in matching colors or styles so you can roll the shots for each cannon weight of a ship at once and tell them apart. Note that pirate frigates have five light cannons so having five D20 of a single style can be beneficial.

### Health
Every ship has two values of health, armor and hull as well as a durability modifier. Armor represents the substantial beating a warship could withstand before the ship begins to suffer significant damage. Hull is the actual integrity of a ship and is a measure of how critically damaged a ship is. The durability modifier is a measure of the likelihood of a ship not being functionally damaged by any given hit.
#### Armor
Armor is separate for both the left and right side of the ship. Armor damage is taken independently meaning that damage sustained to the left of the ship only lowers that armor value and not the right-side armor value. The rear of a ship is considered to have no armor.
#### Durability
Durability represents how resilient a ship is to enemy fire. When a ship is hit, the controller of that ship rolls a number of D20 corresponding to the number of hits rolled by the opponent. The target number for this roll is determined by the type of shot used and the range it was fired from. The appropriate modifier for the ship is added to or subtracted from the number rolled on each die. For every successful durability roll, a single hit is ignored as it is considered to have passed through the ship with negligible impact or to have lodged somewhere of no importance.
Example: 1st Rate ship A fires a 9-cannon broadside achieving 6 hits. In response, ship B makes 6 durability rolls. Two of the durability rolls are successful so ship B takes 6-2=4 damage.
#### Hull
The remaining health stat is Hull. Hull is the integral structure of the ship and every point of damage taken to the hull stat causes a further effect, which is rolled for randomly.
Anytime a ship takes hull damage equal to its hull value, it is considered to have surrendered or become incapacitated and will drift as per the rules for “wrecks.” If a ship ever has two or more hull damage above and beyond its hull points, it is broken up. Ships that are broken up are removed from the board immediately and any cargo or captains are marked as floating in the water within the area of the base of the ship when it was broken up.
#### Cannon
Every ship has a number of cannons determined by its rating and modified by nation and captain. By default, all of the main guns on a ship are standard cannons and can be fired out of either side of the ship. The number of cannons a ship has is the maximum number of shots that ship may fire in a single turn.
Cannons are all one of four different weights: light, medium, heavy, and super heavy. The damage of each cannon is based on the weight of the cannon. The damage is shown in the table below:
| Weight | Standard Damage | Carronade Damage |
| Light | 8 | 12 |
| Medium | 10 | 14 |
| Heavy | 12 | 16 |
| Super Heavy | 14 | 18 |

All cannon shots have -2 damage when firing at extreme range.
Cannons firing standard shot have a critical range of 1 meaning that a dice result matching the target number is counted as a critical hits.
#### Crew
Every ship has a number of crew equal to the “crew” stat. These crew can participate in boarding actions or objective searches and are essential to basic ship function. Crews are selected on a ship-by-ship basis and have different stats based on the crew selected. Crew stats are Gunnery, Boarding, Repair, and Objective. These four skills set the base values for success in each of their respective categories. Gunnery is the base number used when determining the target number for shooting. Boarding is the base number used when determining the target number in boarding actions. Repair is the base number used when determining the target number for a successful removal of damage. Objective is the base number used when determining the target number for an objective search.
Every faction has multiple different crew selections available and each crew type can be selected for any number of ships in a squadron. Each faction has a crew with a points cost of 0 but this crew also has the most basic stat line. The cost for specialized crews are listed in each faction’s section.
The faction specific crews are listed in “Building a Squadron” section of the rules.
##### Crew status effect
Crew status is determined by comparing the current crew total against the starting baseline crew of the ship in question. Extra crew berths provide additional crew but do not count towards the starting baseline crew value.
Example: A 1st Rate Atrytian ship has a base crew value of 9, any crew status is done by comparing the current crew count against 9. If this ship has extra crew berths, it has 4 additional crew to start with, for a total of 13. This ship will only be considered to be at <50% strength when its total crew is reduced to <50% of the base value of 9. This ship would need to lose 9 crew before suffering a crew status penalty as 4/9 is <50%.
Full strength: When a ship has 50% or more of its starting crew value, it may move and perform one other action per activation as normal.
<50% strength: When a ship has less than 50% of its starting crew value, it may either move or perform a different action. The ship may instead move at half speed and fire with half of its cannons rounded down or conduct a crew roll/objective roll at -5 to the target number.
<=25% strength: When a ship has 25% or less of its starting crew value, the ship is abandoned as the remaining crew either surrenders or abandons the ship via lifeboats.
## Playing the Game
### Setting up the Table
The first step of a game of Sail & Broadside is to set up the table. There are two primary ways to do this. The first of which is turning to the scenarios section and selecting a scenario through a random roll or mutually agreeing with your opponent. The scenario’s instructions will provide guidelines on how to set up the terrain, what the objectives are, where the players start, and what special rules, if any, are in effect.
Instead you can play a basic game where you set up the terrain in a mutually agreeable manner. The waters of Sail & Broadside are primarily coastal and as such areas of shallows, small islands, and rocky outcroppings are common so there should be a variety of obstructions around the board. You and your opponent should determine which two opposing board sides are the deployment sides. 
With the table set up and the deployment areas decided on, the initial wind will be determined following the rules in the next section.
#### Wind Direction
Once the table is set up and before deployment begins, the initial wind direction is determined. To prevent either side from being directly disadvantaged by the wind, the wind will never blow directly across the table from one deployment zone to the other.
Before determining initial wind direction, the wind condition for the game is determined by rolling a D20 and consulting the table below.
| Die Roll | Result |
| 1-5 | Calm Wind |
| 6-15 | Fair Weather |
| 16-20 | Rough Seas |

When facing the table from behind one deployment zone, the possible wind directions are numbered from 1 through 6 starting in the near left-hand corner and proceeding clockwise as depicted above.123654Deployment Zone ADeployment Zone B
Roll a D6 and the initial wind is blowing from the number rolled to the opposite side or corner of the table. Example: A 3 is rolled so the wind is blowing evenly across the entire board along the line between point 3 and point 6.
#### Deployment
With the table set up and the initial wind direction determined, it is time to deploy your squadron of ships. Determining which deployment zone belongs to which player is done by each player rolling a D20 for an initiative roll and adding any initiative bonuses to the roll. The higher roller decides which deployment zone is theirs. Deployment is done in alternating turns where you and your opponent each place one ship at a time. The first player to place a ship is the player who chose their deployment zone, or as specified in the scenario. This roll also serves as the initiative roll for the first turn.
### The Turn
Every turn includes the same steps listed below:
Initiative: One player from each side rolls a single D20 and adds the initiative bonuses of their captains. The player with the highest modified total will go first for the turn and activate a single ship. Note: In cases of a tie for initiative, the player with more ships in the “running” orientation to the wind activates first.
Activation: Each player will activate one ship at a time in an alternating sequence between players until all ships have been activated. Unless crew status prevents it, each activation consists of movement and a single action from the following list:
Shooting
Repairing
Boarding
Objective roll
Wind: Any ships that have been defeated but not sunk will drift during this phase and then a single D20 is rolled and compared to the appropriate wind condition table to determine the wind behavior for the next turn.
#### Activation Phase
The activation phase of the turn consists of every ship being activated a single time. These activations alternate between players starting with whichever player had the highest modified initiative roll. Activated ships can be marked with a token to aid in tracking.
Each ship activation consists of two actions, moving and one other action. The actions are listed below.
Moving: Moving the ship up to its wind-modified maximum distance
Shooting: Using the cannons of a ship to fire on an opponent's ships.
Repairing: Making a repair roll to repair hull damage and effects.
Boarding: Resolving a boarding action as described on page xx
Objective: Rolling for port searches, collecting floating debris, any other action needed as part of an objective.
Shooting and repairing can both be done at any time during an activation including midway through movement. Boarding and objective actions must be done before or after moving and cannot be done part way through a move. Each action can only be done once per turn.
#### Wind Phase
The wind phase consists of two portions, Drift and Wind Shift, and occurs after the last ship of the turn has been activated. The drift portion occurs first, followed by the wind shift portion.
##### Drift
During the drift portion, all wrecks are moved 2” in the direction of the wind due to drift. If a drifting ship enters shallows, stop its movement at the point it first enters shallows. For drifting ships, consider the narrow side of the ship nearest to the shallows as the “front” for determining when it has entered the shallows. When a drifting wreck enters rough waters there is no impact. Ships that are not wrecks that are subject to drift do so during their activation, as described in Movement.
##### Wind Shift
During the wind shift portion, one player rolls a D20 and consults the appropriate wind table below to determine the results.
There are three standard wind conditions and each game of Sail & Broadside is played under a single wind condition. The wind condition is determined as described in the Wind Direction portion of Playing the Game. With the wind condition known, a single D20 is rolled against the appropriate table below.
For wind shifts, roll a D20 with the result of 1-10 resulting in a shift counterclockwise while an 11-20 results in a shift clockwise.
##### Calm Wind
| Die Roll | Result |
| 1-5 | There is no wind as the seas are becalmed. |
| 6-17 | The wind remains steady in its current direction. |
| 18-20 | The wind shifts mildly, roll a die to determine clockwise or counterclockwise for the shift. |

##### Fair Weather

### Game End
A game of Sail & Broadside lasts six turns. At the end of the 6th turn, objective points are totalled and a winner is determined. The game can end early if a player is Routed. The rules for routing are explained in the next section.
#### Routed
Due to the extreme cost of ships and value of experienced sailors, fights among sailing ships are rarely conducted to the bitter end. It is far more common for captains and admirals to decide they cannot win and, if possible, to extract what is left of their squadron.
In game terms, if a player has equal to or fewer than 1/2 of the game point total in points remaining at the start of their turn, they will be routed. The game ends at the end of the turn that had any player routed. During this final turn no special rules apply but it is a good time to make a last ditch play for objective points as the squadrons scramble to complete their missions before fleeing the scene. Point totals for retreat are always rounded up and include both ship and upgrade/captain & crew points. 
Example: In your typical 300 point game there are 250 ship points and 50 points of upgrades/captains/crew. In such a game if a player has 150 or fewer points remaining, they are routed and the turn that is beginning is the final turn.
Ships captured through boarding do not count as points left alive. When calculating points for routing, only the original ship and associated upgrades/crew/officers is considered. 
Example 1: During boarding Player A captures a ship and moves their full crew onto it. The original ship is abandoned. This new ship is worth points for routing purposes equal to the original ship’s total points.
Example 2: During boarding, Player A captures a ship and splits their crew between it and their original ship. The new ship is worth 0 points when calculating points for routing. The original ship is still worth its total points.
## Movement
Movement for a sailing ship is different than it is for persons on foot. The alignment of a sailing ship to the wind has a significant effect on a sailing ship. Great ships are not capable of turning on the spot either. To represent this in game, each ship has a minimum turning arc. This arc is the minimum arc length needed for the ship to make a 90 degree turn. The three turn lengths are 3, 4, and 5 inches and they each comprise a quarter circle. Each ship may perform any combination of turns or straight sailing in a single movement as desired provided the total length moved does not exceed the modified movement value.
Your maximum move distance for an activation is determined at the start of your move. If you choose to repair prior to moving, your repaired movement value can be used as the basis for your move action.
While ships have a minimum turn radius they can make turns at any more broad curvatures as well. Using a larger arc than normal may sometimes be a convenient method of handling this.
Due to the nature of floating vessels and sails, a ship cannot easily stay in place without being docked. A ship must always move 2”, or its full movement if its modified movement is less than 2”. If, for whatever reason, you elect not to move as described, your ship will drift 2” in the direction of the wind.
A ship can either move forward or backward in an activation but not both. Ships moving backwards have a flat movement value of ¼ of their current base movement value. Backward movement is never modified by the wind.
Any time that the movement of a ship would bring the front edge of its base, or result in a portion of the ship’s base ending the movement in contact with land, the ship stops immediately before that point. In this case only, a full movement activation can be used on a subsequent turn to turn the ship up to 90 degrees to face one long side tangential to the land. This move must be done to the side that minimizes the turn needed.
#### Scraping
When two ships make contact during a move, the moving ship is immediately stopped. The next time either ship involved in a scrape is activated, that ship makes a repair roll before moving. In the case of a successful repair roll the ship is not entangled and may safely move unhindered. In the case of a failed repair roll, the ship is entangled and can only move at half speed as the crew is carefully extricating the ship while attempting their maneuvers. This repair roll does not count against that ship’s activation.
### Wind Attitude
A ship’s alignment to the wind is referred to as its attitude. There are four attitudes; reaching, running, beating, and against. When the wind is less than 30 degrees off either side of the stern of the ship the ship is considered running, this conveys no modifier to movement speed. When the wind is 30-90 degrees off either side of the stern of a ship it is considered reaching, in this attitude the ship's speed is 1.5 times its normal movement. When the wind is 30-90 degrees off either side of the bow of the ship the ship is beating, in this attitude ships may only move at ½ of their normal movement. When the wind is less than 30 degrees off either side of the bow the ship is against the wind and can only move at ¼ its normal move rate.
A ship’s attitude at the beginning of a turn determines its movement for the entire turn. Position yourself at the end of your move well.

#### Rough Water
Ships moving through areas defined as “rough water” must take a rough water check. To complete this check, the player rolls a d20 and compares the results to the table below. 
| Ship Size | Slowed | Stuck |
| 1st Rate | 20 | - |
| 2nd Rate | 18-19 | 20 |
| 3rd Rate | 16-18 | 19-20 |
| 4th Rate | 15-18 | 19-20 |
| 5th Rate | 13-17 | 18-20 |
| Frigate | 11-17 | 18-20 |

Every inch moved by a ship that is “Slowed” costs double from the moment the front edge of the ship’s base enters the rough water until the front edge exits the rough water. A result of “Stuck” causes the ship to immediately stop at the point where the front edge of the ship first contacts the rough water. Any rough water checks due to drifting wrecks during the drift phase are automatically passed.
A check must be made any time a ship enters rough water, or begins its activation in rough water. In the case of a ship moving backwards, the rear edge of the ship is the edge considered for checking for rough water or shallows.
#### Shallows
Shallows are areas around shores and reefs, every size ship considers a different water level to be shallows. In game, shallows are the space around agreed upon terrain pieces equal to the width of that ship's base. This distance is different for each ship type and this is intended as each ship rating has a different draft depth.
When a ship enters shallows for any reason, make a check using the following table:
| Ship Size | Slowed | Stuck |
| 1st Rate | 14-17 | 18-20 |
| 2nd Rate | 15-17 | 18-20 |
| 3rd Rate | 16-18 | 19-20 |
| 4th Rate | 17-18 | 19-20 |
| 5th Rate | 18-19 | 20 |
| Frigate | 20 | - |

A successful roll on the shallows table results in the ship moving at normal speed as the crew carefully avoids obstacles in the water. A ship with a result of “Slowed” has all movement, from the moment the front edge of their base enters the shallow water until the front edge exits the shallow water, cost double normal. A result of “Stuck” on the roll ends the ship's move immediately as the crew have unexpectedly found themselves snagged on an underwater hazard or even momentarily stuck due to a particularly shallow area.
A stuck ship simply loses any remaining movement but is otherwise unaffected. A stuck ship has no impacts from drifting regardless of distance moved. The turn after being stuck is treated exactly as any other turn with the controlling player simply taking a Shallow water check before its move. Shallow water checks must be made every time the leading edge of a ship’s base enters shallow water or begins its turn in shallow waters. In the case of a ship moving backwards, the rear edge of the ship is the edge considered for checking for rough water or shallows.
### Wrecks/Hulls
During encounters on the high seas it is unusual for ships to be outright destroyed. The more common result is for a crew to surrender once their ship has sustained too much damage. Despite being less common, it is not unheard of for engagements to result in one of the ships sustaining enough damage to cause it to break up into wreckage. To represent this in game, a ship can be defeated or become a wreck.
While a ship is defeated when it has 0 hull points remaining, it can happen that damage exceeds this if several points of hull damage occur from a single round of shooting or if a ship is fired upon after it has been defeated.
Defeated: A ship is defeated when it has 0 points of hull damage remaining, this is the result of the crew surrendering as their ship is incapacitated. Contact with a defeated ship due to drift or movement is treated as scraping. Movement is halted at the point of contact and a repair roll must be taken when the defeated ship would drift to determine if the ships are entangled. The repair roll is taken by the active ship using their crew stat including any modifiers.
Wreck: A ship becomes a wreck when its hull value is -2 or below. A wreck is immediately removed from the board as the ship suffered damage sufficient to cause it to break up and sink below the waves.
Drift: Defeated ships will drift every turn in the direction of the wind at 1.5” per turn. Any defeated ship that enters shallows will complete that drift and remain there for the rest of the game or until turned into a wreck.
## Crew Rolls
Crew roll is a general term that can refer to boarding, shooting, repair, or objective searches. This is just a more general term that may be used throughout these rules instead of specifically saying “shooting roll” or “boarding roll” etc. For all crew rolls, a successful roll is one where the rolled number is equal to or less than the target number. Results of exactly the target number are considered critical successes. Modifiers that extend critical range extend the critical range downward. The target number for crew rolls come from the values of the crew selected for the ship being used and are altered by modifiers that apply in the specific situation at hand.
Example: A Korgenal player has Masterful Repairs which extends the critical range of their repair rolls by +2. When rolling to repair with a crew repair value of 14, a roll of 14, 13, or 12 would all be considered critical successes.
### Shooting
The large cannons capable of launching a cannonball across the waves to smash into an enemy ship are truly massive. To handle the weight and recoil of these destructive beasts, they must be mounted on sturdy decks with tracks to roll back along after a shot. Pulleys and ropes are then used to hoist the cannon forward for the next shot..
Shooting in game can be done at any point during a move. A target ship must be within your line of fire in order to fire on them. To determine the line of fire, extend an imaginary line along the centerline of your ship’s base. If that line intersects any part of the base of your target, you can shoot. Range is determined by measuring from the center of the nearest long side of your ship’s base to the nearest point of the target ship. There will be situations where an opposing ship is just barely in your line of fire, you are still able to shoot at them to full effect as the cannons of large sailing ships have a small degree of rotation in their berths. This mechanic reflects that.
After establishing line of fire, determine if more or less than half of the target ship’s base is visible from the center of your ship’s centerline nearest the target.
If less than half of the target ship’s base is visible, the target is considered to be “obstructed.”
If more than half of the target ship’s base is visible, the shooting proceeds with no obstructed modifier.
Shooting is simply rolling a single D20 per cannon on your ship with all dice rolls equal to or less than your modified gunnery score resulting in a hit. As with all crew rolls, rolls exceeding your target number are failures, rolls equal to your target number are criticals, rolls below your target number are successes. During shooting your crew's gunnery score will be modified by the range based on the type of cannon fired, any bonuses from your faction or captain, and a number of factors of the targeted ship.
Shooting follows the steps below:
Declare the point during movement that shooting occurs and the target of the shooting.
Determine line of fire and range.
If the target is not in range or not in your line of fire, your shots miss.
Complete any movement remaining.
Roll shots.
Determine hits.
Target ship rolls durability with each success cancelling out one hit.
Apply any remaining hits to armor, or hull if no armor remains.
A targeted ship is considered “head on” or “tail on” if the line extending from your centerline intersects the front or rear narrow side of your target’s base.
Note: If the firing ship cannot see at least half of the target ship’s base without intersecting the front or rear narrow side of the target’s base, the shot is considered “obstructed.”

#### Special Shots
Carronades and standard cannons are both capable of firing special shots. To fire these shots simply declare that your cannons are firing one of the following shot types; grapeshot or ball & chain. 
Resolving shots with these special shots is similar to regular shooting with a few differences. Special shots do not cause any armor or hull damage. These shots instead cause loss of crew or reduction in base movement. All hits from special shots can be rolled against for durability. The durability modifier does not apply to special shots. The damage of special shots is determined as usual, based on cannon weight and modified by carronades and extreme range.
Ball & Chain: This shot consists of two small cannon balls attached together with a length of chain. The whirling nature and shape of this shot causes it to be an effective means of damaging masts, sails, and rigging. Every hit with a failed durability roll of this type results in a one inch reduction to target ship movement.
Grapeshot: This shot consists of a mass of small metal balls packed tightly in a canvas bag that spreads when fired and has a devastating effect on any unfortunate soul caught in the blast. Every hit with a failed durability roll of this type results in a crew death.

#### Damage
After shooting has been completed, the target ship makes a durability roll for every hit scored. Durability rolls are simply a D20 roll plus any modifiers from durability. For a durability roll to succeed, the number rolled must equal or exceed the damage of the shot.
When a critical hit is rolled, the shot causes an immediate point of damage and also requires the rolling of a durability roll. If the durability roll is failed, the critical hit results in a total of two damage. If the durability roll is successful, the critical hit results in a total of one damage. In this way, a critical hit always results in at least one damage. This shot hits in just the right spot to damage something important. Missed shots result in no damage.
Every point of hull damage has a significant impact on the ship and has a high likelihood of imparting a negative effect. For each point of hull damage sustained roll once on the “Damage Effect” table on page XX.
### Repairs
Some damage effects can be repaired after a success on a repair roll but others are either irreparable or require a ship upgrade to be able to be repaired. Repair rolls are a single D20 roll using the crew's repair stat as the target value. A successful repair roll is one that is equal to or less than the target value. When a repair roll is successful, a single point of hull damage is repaired and a damage effect can be removed. Which damage effect is to be removed must be stated before the roll as some upgrades give bonuses to repair rolls depending on damage effect chosen. In the case of a critical success on a repair roll, one additional hull damage or effect may be removed. Each successful repair roll can be used to restore up to 2” of movement lost to ball & chain in place of a damage effect.
Damage effects that can be repaired are fire, flooding, and movement reduction from ball & chain shot. Damaged guns and damaged rigging effects can be repaired when the appropriate ship upgrade is present. The first result of damaged gun ports can be ignored with the proper upgrade. Crew lost to damage effects, grapeshot, boarding, or any other means, can not be recovered during a game.
Fire: The fire damage effect causes one point of hull damage to be inflicted at the end of each activation of the ship that is on fire, until the fire is put out. A successful repair roll ends the fire status before the damage for that turn is done. This successful repair roll also removes a single hull damage. Hull damage caused by fire does not cause damage effects. In the case of multiple fires, one hull point is removed per fire. A single successful repair roll will end all fires.
Flooding: A flooding damage effect causes an immediate “Taking on Water” effect which reduces the ship’s unmodified sail speed by 1” per effect. Flooding causes an additional “Taking on Water” effect at the end of the flooding ship’s activation each turn. A successful repair removes a single “Taking on Water” effect and ends the flooding state. Any ship reduced to 1” of movement or less from “Taking on Water” is considered defeated, regardless of how earlier movement reductions were caused. That ship will be removed from the board during the drift phase on the second turn after its defeat. In the case of multiple flooding states, a single successful repair roll will end all flooding states but will only remove a single “Taking on Water” effect.

### Boarding
While often not the best approach, captains may occasionally decide to board an enemy vessel. Boarding occurs when two ships make contact with each other and one of the captains decides to board.
To initiate boarding in game you must move a ship into contact with a long side of another. The contacting ship will rotate upon contact to have one side of its base flat along one of the long sides of the target. This rotation is automatic, cannot exceed 90 degrees, does not require turn arcs, does not count toward the distance moved, and is always to the side that minimizes the rotation of the moving ship.
Once boarding contact is made both players roll a number of D20 equal to their crew number. The target number for boarding is based on your crew stats plus any applicable boarding bonuses. Compare the total successes from each side, criticals count as two, the player with fewer successes loses crew equal to the difference in total successes.
Example: Player 1 conducts a boarding move against Player 2’s ship. Player 1 and Player 2 each have 8 crewmen. They both make 8 boarding rolls, Player 1 has 5 successful rolls to Player 2’s 3. Player 2 then loses 2 crewmen.
Boarding ends after this single round. During the activation for each ship you can either simply move apart, or you can conduct another round of boarding combat. If a boarding action results in either ship’s crew being below 25% of the ship's starting crew, that ship's crew surrenders. When a crew surrenders, the ship is considered lost and the attacker may seize the vessel by moving crew from their ship to the surrendered ship. Both ships must have at least 25% of their respective base crew value after the move. If a player decides not to split their crew they may choose which ship to use for the remainder of the game. This transfer of crew does count as the activation for both ships and they cannot be activated again this turn. If the defending ship had already been activated that turn, the movement of crew can still be completed.
Whenever a ship is acquired during boarding, the captain of the initial ship can decide which ship he/she will captain. The other ship will then be commanded by a junior officer with no bonus and the crew stats of the boarding crew.
Multiple ships can be involved in a boarding action. To use multiple ships in a single boarding, both ships will be considered activated for the turn. Only one ship activated this way may be moved, the other ship must already be in contact with the opposing ship. This activation allows a single ship to move into contact with an opposing ship and conduct a boarding round using the total of both crews. This contribution of boarding dice is the only action the other ship may take as its activation.
### Objective Searches
Objective searches can be conducted before or after movement while a ship is within the shallows of an island, in contact with a ship, or in the area of a wreck being searched. To initiate an objective search you must move a ship into the appropriate area for the object being searched. 
In the case of a ship search, the contacting ship will rotate upon contact to have one side of its base flat along one of the long sides of the target. This rotation is automatic, cannot exceed 90 degrees, does not require turn arcs, does not count toward the distance moved, and is always to the side that minimizes the rotation of the moving ship.
Searches of wreckage can be done before or after movement so long as some part of the ship is in the wreckage area.
For searching an island, the searching ship must have any part of its base within the shallows of an island or within 1” if there are no shallows.
## Building a Squadron
Before you can play a game of Sail & Broadside, each player must create a squadron. This is often referred to in miniatures as “building a list.” Creating a squadron is done by first choosing a point value for the game. These rules are designed around a point total of 250 ship points with 50 upgrade points for a total game points of 300 pts. These values can be adjusted up or down as players choose. The ratio of 5 ship points per upgrade point should be maintained in most situations. Once a point value has been decided on, each player shall:
Choose a nation
Select ships totaling points up to the agreed upon ship points.
Select crews, captains/bonuses, and upgrades for their ships up to the total of the upgrade points. Any unused ship points can be used as additional upgrade points.
Note: Unused upgrade points cannot be used as additional ship points.
### Choosing a Nation
There are four main factions sailing the seas for players to choose from when building a squadron. The four factions are the Atrytian Empire, the Korgenal Principality, the Denrudain Republic, and a loosely organized collection of pirates.
#### Atrytian Empire
The Atrytian Empire focuses on close range brawling. They believe that the most effective way to defeat the enemy is to pull close alongside the enemy and pummel them with broadsides. Their preferred tactics have led some of their captains to upgrade their ships with very heavy timber. This tendency allows their ships to take significantly more cannon fire while maintaining seaworthiness. The captains and admirals from the Atrytian Empire have a tendency to bring heavier guns. Many captains have also spent considerable fortunes outfitting their ships with additional gunpowder at personal expense. This additional gunpowder is used to allow the Atrytian sailors actual live-fire drills resulting in more reliable close-range firing.
Atrytian captains have a passion for close-in fighting where their larger heavier ships can prove their strength in a flat-out brawl. Captains of the Atrytian Empire tend towards a reliance on sturdy large ships with the greatest weight of shot they can bring to bear in a broadside.

### Upgrades
Every ship can have upgrades purchased using the upgrades points pool, which includes any unused points from the ship point pool. There are three types of upgrades in normal games of Sail & Broadside: Captains/Admirals, Ship Upgrades, and Crews.
#### Captains
Every ship must have a captain, though some have no cost. If no captain bonuses are chosen, the ship is assumed to be commanded by a junior officer with no particular area of expertise. Each faction has access to general officers as well as a small pool of particularly renowned officers. The following costs apply for choosing all general captains:
Junior Line Officer: 0 pts, no bonus.
Line Officer: 2 pts for a common bonus from a primary idea group.
4 pts for a rare bonus from a primary idea group.
4 pts for a common bonus from a secondary idea group.
6 pts for a rare bonus from a secondary idea group.
Senior Line Officer: 2 pts additional to give a line officer a +2 modifier to initiative rolls.
##### Idea Groups
Currently in the realm of sailing ship combat there are eight major idea groups. Each faction focuses their doctrine around two of these schools of thought and as such most of their captains have experience in these styles of fighting. While it is uncommon, captains do foray beyond the primary idea groups of their faction and as such each faction has access to bonuses from an additional three secondary idea groups.
In game terms, a standard line officer of any faction can be trained with a single combat bonus from a single idea group. Common bonuses from within either of a faction’s primary idea groups are 2 points while rare bonuses are 4 points. Bonuses from a faction’s secondary idea groups cost an additional 2 points. In addition to the cost, each combat bonus has limited availability within a squadron. For primary idea groups, the availability is 4 for each of the common bonuses and 2 for each of the rare bonuses. For secondary idea groups, availability is limited to 2 for each common bonus and 1 for each rare bonus. The availability of a bonus is the maximum number of officers in each squadron with that specific bonus.
Line Officers by default have an initiative value of 0, for an additional 2 points they can be upgraded to Senior Line Officers with an initiative value of +2.

### Ship Upgrades
Ships have a number of upgrade options that can be bought before the start of a game. The ship upgrades available are the same for every nation. Every upgrade can only be taken once per ship. Ship upgrades use one cargo space each unless otherwise noted.
When both players are done selecting ships and allocating upgrade points, the squadron creation is complete. Typically squadron lists will be made before selecting a scenario to play.
Heavy Timbers: 1st rate: 7pts, 2nd rate: 6pts, 3rd rate: 5pts, 4th rate: 4pts, 5th rate: 3pts, Frigate: 3pts
Heavy timbers add a +2 modifier to durability rolls for any ship constructed with heavy timbers. The thicker timbers and increased size and number of structural members causes this upgrade to reduce the cargo of the modified ship by one.
Heavy timbers use one cargo space on the ship and cannot be taken if there are no cargo spaces free.
Extra Rigging: 1st & 2nd rate: 3pts, 3rd & 4th rate: 2pts, 5th rate & Frigate: 1pt
Extra rigging allows the crew to repair the effect of “Damaged Rigging”, or to restore up to 4” of movement that has been lost to ball & chain fire. Without this upgrade, "Damaged Rigging" cannot be repaired and only 2" of movement lost to ball & chain is repaired per repair roll.
Extra rigging uses one cargo space on the ship and cannot be taken if there are no cargo spaces available.
Extra Cannons: 1st & 2nd rate: 3pts, 3rd & 4th rate: 2pts, 5th rate & Frigate: 1pt
Extra Cannon allows the crew to repair the effect of “Damaged Guns” twice per game. A failed repair roll does not expend a use of this upgrade. Without this upgrade "Damaged Guns" cannot be repaired.
Extra cannon uses one cargo space on the ship and cannot be taken if there are no cargo spaces available.
Extra Crew Berths: 1st & 2nd rate: 4pts, 3rd & 4th rate: 3pts, 5th rate & Frigate: 2pts
Extra crew berths add 4 crew to 1st & 2nd rate ships, 3 crew to 3rd & 4th rate ships, and 2 crew to 5th rate & Frigate ships. Extra crew berths use one cargo space on the ship and cannot be taken if there are no cargo spaces available.
Reinforced Gun Ports: 1st & 2nd rate: 3pts, 3rd & 4th rate: 2pts, 5th rate & Frigate: 1pt
Reinforced gun ports allows the upgraded ship to ignore the first “Gun Ports Damaged” hull result as the timber around each gun port has been reinforced and layered. This layering causes the equipment necessary for the cannons to be stored further inside the ship, reducing the effective cargo space by one for the upgraded ship.
Reinforced gun ports use one cargo space on the ship and cannot be taken if there are no cargo spaces available. This effect is a one time use upgrade per game.
Extra Buckets: 1st & 2nd rate: 3pts, 3rd & 4th rate: 2pts, 5th rate & Frigate: 1pt
Extra buckets confer a +2 modifier to the target number of repair rolls taken against flooding or fire.
Extra buckets, while helpful, do not take up much space on a ship and therefore do not use a cargo space. This bonus applies to all such repair rolls in a game.
Carronades: 1 pt per cannon
Carronades are a heavy hitting cannon with worse accuracy. By shortening the cannon, reducing the powder used in every shot, and reducing the cannon wall thickness, a larger shot can be fired from the same weight cannon. In game terms a carronade changes the damage of the cannon by +4. Carronades change the range table for the cannons to the range table below:
| Range | Point Blank | Near | Middle | Far | Extreme |
| Distance (in) | 0-4 | 4-8 | 8-12 | 12-16 | 16-20 |
| Gunnery mod. | +3 | +1 | -2 | -5 | -8 |

All cannons of a given weight must be upgraded to carronades as a group. For example, if a player wishes to upgrade the three heavy cannons of their ship, they must pay 3pts and upgrade all three heavy cannons to heavy carronades. The player cannot upgrade a portion of the cannons of the chosen weight.
## Scenarios
While Sail & Broadside can readily be played in a simple “deathmatch” manner, the rules are designed to be enjoyed in scenario play. The goals and foci of scenarios allow a wider range of ships and tactics to be of value and greatly increase the replayability and immersion of games. The following scenarios represent some of the various engagements in which the competing nations find themselves embroiled. Both players agreeing on a scenario, rolling randomly for a scenario, or playing through a preset group of scenarios such as in an escalation league or a tournament are all excellent ways to enjoy Sail & Broadside. Escalation leagues where the number of points per game begin low and increase game after game, are an excellent way to ease players into the rules while being an immersive representation of the increasing scope and scale of conflict between the nations as they become more invested in the region.
### Secret Objectives
Secret objectives are a list of objectives that remains the same regardless of what scenario is being played. For a typical scenario each player will roll twice from the secret objective chart. If any result is rolled multiple times, re-roll until this is not the case. Players may also elect to draw cards from an objective deck provided one is available.
After each player has secretly rolled two different results, they each choose one objective to be their secret objective for the game. Completing this objective is worth points based on the scenario description. Some scenarios may entail rolling and selecting a different number of objectives.

### Squadron Engagement
Two opposing squadrons have chanced upon each other while patrolling in the region. With no significant objectives in the area, the main objective is to hinder the opposing force as severely as possible. Perhaps heavy losses here will slow their efforts in the region.
Setting up the Scenario:
Setup the terrain on the board as normal using a scattering of scenery to obstruct movement and sight. There are no special terrain requirements for this scenario.
Deployment zones for this scenario are the standard 12” from the board edge on opposite sides of the board.
For this mission both players roll three times on the secret objective table and choose two. After rolling to determine which deployment side is whose, both players alternate deploying ships.
Objective Points:
Both Players:
2 pts* for destroying 50-99 pts (⅙th-⅓rd) of the opponent’s squadron
4 pts* for destroying 100-149 pts (⅓rd-½) of the opponent
6 pts* for destroying 150-300 pts (>½) of the opponent
2 pts for destroying the opposing flagship
1 pt per successful secret objective, two are selected in this scenario.
*In this scenario you get points for the highest level of points destroyed, the ship destruction points are not cumulative.
### Their Goals are Unknown
The mission is clear, what is not clear is what their mission is. Two squadrons under different commands have come into contact and there is no clear strategic objective in the region, but it seems both sides have something they were sent to do.
Setting up the Scenario:

Deployment zones for this scenario are the standard 12” from the board edge on opposite sides of the board. After rolling to determine which deployment side is whose, both players alternate deploying ships.
For this scenario both players will roll four times on the secret objective table and choose three of the objectives to comprise their mission.
Objective Points:
Both Players:

### The Cargo Must be Recovered
A ship carrying very valuable cargo was left adrift due to a terrible storm. We have word that the cargo on board is well worth risking the storm to recover. It is safe to assume that others in the area will reach the same conclusion. The cargo must be recovered, failing that, it must not fall into the hands of our enemies.
Setting up the Scenario:
Setup the terrain on the board as normal using a scattering of scenery to obstruct movement and sight. In the center of the board place a ship of 2nd through 4th rate, the size can be agreed upon by the players. This ship drifts 1.5” per turn in the direction of the wind during the drift phase.
Deployment zones for this scenario are the standard 12” from the board edge on opposite sides of the board. After rolling to determine which deployment side is whose, both players alternate deploying ships.
The weather condition for this scenario is a squall every turn, roll for a new random wind direction after every turn.
Optional Rule: During squalls all ships between 1st and 4th rate cannot use their heaviest class of cannons safely due to the heaving seas lapping at the gun ports. Ships may attempt to use their heaviest cannons but must roll a D20 after firing per cannon fired. This roll is made with a target number of 17, each failed roll causes a single “Taking on Water” effect. These effects can be removed through repair rolls using the rules for repair rolls.
Example: A player chooses to fire with their 1st rate ship including its four super heavy cannons. After resolving their shots, the player rolls four D20s and applies “Taking on Water” for each 18-20 rolled.
Objective Points:
Both Players:
2 pts for being the first player to succeed on an objective roll while in contact with the center ship, doing so loads the cargo into the ship. It can be lost as per normal rules.
6 pts for holding the recovered cargo at the end of the game.
2 pts for the player who sank the last ship to hold the cargo if nobody has the cargo onboard at the end of the game.
1 pt for sinking the opponent’s flagship
1 pt for completing the secret objective.

### Prisoner Exchange Gone Awry
A meeting was arranged to exchange captive captains from two different factions. Unfortunately, as the time of the swap approached, negotiations broke down and shots were fired. Recover your captain while keeping the opponent's captain secure.
Game Setup:
Before deployment each player marks on their squadron list which ship holds the captive captain and does not reveal this information. Scenery is set up with no special requirements.
Deployment is done with the usual 12" deployment zones.
Discovering the Captive:
To determine if a ship holds the captive, you must succeed on an objective roll during a ship’s activation while within 20" of the target ship. Objective rolls made between 0”-12” are unmodified. Rolls between 12”-20” have a -3 modifier. An objective roll made while in contact with the ship have a +6 modifier. If the objective roll succeeds, the other player must reveal whether or not the captive is on that ship. This objective roll can be taken at any point during the turn and is measured like a shooting action.
Objective Points:
Both Players:
2 pts for identifying the opposing ship holding your captain prisoner.
4 pts for retrieving the captive captain via a successful round of boarding or scooping from the water.
2 pts for retaining control of the opponent's captain at the end of the game.
1 pt for defeating the opponent’s flagship through hull damage or crew loss.
1 pt for completing your secret objective.
### Treasure Trove
Reports have identified a large treasure trove is hidden in this area of sea. All forces in the area are likely to be rushing to secure this treasure, take control of the treasure and get away.
Game Setup:
Place at least three islands of 4” or larger along the table centerline. They do not need to be centered but should be distributed in a way that both players agree on. As with all scenarios, additional scenery can make for a more interesting game.
Deployment is done with the usual 12" deployment zones.
Finding the Treasure Trove:
Before the initiative roll for turn two, roll a die to randomly determine a centerline island, this island contains three valuable treasure objects such as chests of gold, silver, gemstones, or even much needed supplies.
Before the initiative roll of turn four, randomly determine another centerline island, different from the first, this island contains one remaining valuable treasure chest.
Objective Points:
Both Players:
2 pts per treasure chest retrieved.
1 pt for defeating the opponent’s flagship through hull damage or crew loss.
1 pt for completing your secret objective.
